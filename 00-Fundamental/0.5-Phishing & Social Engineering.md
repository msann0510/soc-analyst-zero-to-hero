# 📖 What is Social Engineering?
Social Engineering is the practice of manipulating people into revealing sensitive information or performing actions that benefit an attacker.
Instead of exploiting software vulnerabilities, attackers exploit human psychology such as trust, fear, urgency, or curiosity.

---

# 🎣 What is Phishing?
Phishing is a type of Social Engineering attack where attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to steal sensitive information.
Common targets include:
- Usernames
- Passwords
- MFA Codes
- Credit Card Information
- Company Data
Phishing is one of the most common methods attackers use to gain Initial Access.

---

# Initial Access
Initial Access is the stage where an attacker first gains access to a victim's system or account.
Common Initial Access methods include:

- Phishing Emails
- Malicious Attachments
- Fake Login Pages
- Weak Passwords

---

# Common Phishing Types
### 🌐 Phishing
A generic phishing campaign sent to many victims.

### 👥 Spear Phishing
A targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific person or organization.

### 🐳 Whaling
A phishing attack targeting executives or high-value individuals.

### 💬 Smishing
Phishing conducted through SMS messages.

### 📞 Vishing
Phishing conducted through voice calls.

### 📩 Business Email Compromise (BEC)
A phishing attack where attackers impersonate trusted business contacts to trick victims into transferring money or revealing confidential information.

---

# Email Components Used in Phishing

## 🪛 Email Attachments
Email attachments are files included in an email.
Common file types:
- PDF
- DOCX
- XLSX
- ZIP
- EXE
Attackers often use malicious attachments to deliver malware.

---

## 🔗 URL (Uniform Resource Locator)
A URL is the address used to access resources on the internet.
Attackers frequently create malicious URLs that closely resemble legitimate websites.
Example:
Legitimate:
```text
https://login.microsoft.com
```
Malicious:
```text
https://login-microsoft-support.com
```

---

## 🏠 Domain
A Domain is the human-readable name of a website.
Attackers often register look-alike domains to impersonate trusted organizations.
Example
Legitimate:
```text
microsoft.com
```
Malicious:
```text
micr0soft-login.com
```

# How Phishing Works
```text
Attacker
    │
    ▼
Creates Fake Email
    │
    ▼
Victim Receives Email
    │
    ▼
Victim Clicks Link
    │
    ▼
Fake Login Page
    │
    ▼
Credentials Stolen
```

---

# Indicator of Compromise (IOC)
An Indicator of Compromise (IOC) is evidence that suggests a system or account may have been compromised.
Common phishing IOCs include:
- Suspicious sender addresses
- Fake domains
- Malicious URLs
- Unexpected email attachments
- Malicious IP addresses
- File hashes
- Newly registered domains
SOC Analysts use IOCs to identify affected users, detect related attacks, and block future threats.

---

# Security Controls
The following controls help reduce phishing attacks:
- Security Awareness Training
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Email Filtering
- Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)
- Domain Protection
- Regular Security Updates

---

# Why Phishing Matters
Phishing remains one of the most successful attack techniques because it targets human behavior instead of technical vulnerabilities.
Many ransomware infections, credential theft incidents, and Business Email Compromise (BEC) attacks begin with a successful phishing email.
Understanding phishing helps SOC Analysts detect suspicious emails, investigate incidents, and reduce organizational risk.

---

# 📌 Key Takeaways
- Social Engineering targets people rather than technology.
- Phishing is one of the most common Initial Access techniques.
- Different phishing attacks target different victims and communication channels.
- URLs, domains, attachments, and sender addresses should always be verified.
- IOC analysis helps SOC Analysts identify compromised systems.
- User awareness and layered security controls significantly reduce phishing risks.
